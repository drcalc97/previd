| Item | Scope |
|---|---|
| Core tests | Timed Up and Go (TUG), Five-Times Sit-to-Stand (5xSTS), Four-Metre Gait Speed (4mGS) |

## 1. Intended-purpose implications of adding measurement

`governance/01` states that PreVid gives a "visual impression" that supplements written information, and that it does **not** score, measure or interpret (section 2: "No frailty scoring, diagnosis, triage, risk prediction or recommendation"). `governance/11` question 5 already flags that "measurement (timers, frame-by-frame…) could change the analysis". This module crosses that line deliberately. Implications:

1. **The product stops being only "store, transmit, display".** It will (a) capture a clinician-asserted timing, (b) compute numeric results, (c) store and trend them. Section 2 of `governance/01` is no longer accurate for this module and must not be left unchanged. Wording options (for CSO/regulatory consultant, not decided here): the module is described as a *recording and arithmetic aid for results that a clinician derives*, versus as a *measurement or assessment tool*. The choice has regulatory consequences (see `governance/13`). **[External review]**
2. **A displayed number acquires authority a video does not have.** Video is a "visual impression"; a number such as "TUG 14.2 s" or "SPPB 7/12" invites comparison, thresholds and anchoring. Core hazard H04 (over-reliance) rises in likelihood for this module (see PPA-H12).
3. **Accuracy claims become possible, and therefore testable.** A video-derived time carries a quantisation and timing error that the manual stopwatch method does not carry in the same way. The product must state what error is expected and must not imply stopwatch-equivalent accuracy until that is evidenced (PPA-H03, PPA-H04). Technical validation of timing and clinical validation of the workflow are **separate evidence streams** (see `governance/13`, section 5).
4. **Source of truth.** The live stopwatch time and the video-derived time are two different measurements of one event. The product must define which one is the "result", and show the other without inventing agreement (PPA-H24).
5. **Derived estimates (DASI → VO2peak) are more than a score.** An estimate of a physiological quantity from a questionnaire is a clinical inference, not a display of data. It should be treated as the highest-scrutiny feature in the module. Option for the CSO: ship DASI total only and defer the VO2peak estimate. **[External review]**
6. **Longitudinal charts imply "trend".** A chart plus a line implies improvement or decline. Without a defined comparability rule (same protocol, same version, same deviations) a trend is a clinical claim (PPA-H09, PPA-H10, PPA-H23).
7. **Out-of-scope boundary must be written down and enforced in code review.** No thresholds, colouring by value, "normal/abnormal" labels, risk or frailty categories, percentile/reference-range comparison, fitness or surgical-suitability language, or alerts. Any later addition re-opens this assessment and `governance/11`. **[Planned]** change-control gate; see core hazard H22.
8. **Intended users and settings change.** Previously MDT reviewers viewed a clip. Now clinicians also need training in the protocols and in the editor. The module presumes the recording clinician is competent to administer the test; PreVid does not teach or verify that (PPA-H05, PPA-H14). **[NHS approval]**
9. **Existing owner rules still bind.** Consent at every recording; patients exist only with a recording (ADR-016/017); org admins can view and delete video (ADR-014). Derived data (results, timing marks, audit history) inherits these constraints (PPA-H22, PPA-H26).
10. **Proposed intended-purpose addendum (draft, for CSO review; not agreed):** "The Physical Performance Assessment module lets an authorised clinician record a patient performing a standard physical performance test, mark start and end frames, and have the application perform arithmetic on clinician-entered or clinician-marked values. The clinician remains responsible for test selection, patient suitability, safe administration, validity of the result and its interpretation. The module does not interpret results, categorise patients, predict risk or indicate fitness for surgery."

## 2. Risk scale (identical to `governance/02`)

**Likelihood (L)**: 1 Very unlikely · 2 Unlikely · 3 Possible · 4 Likely · 5 Very likely.
**Severity (S)**: 1 Minor · 2 Low (minor delay/short-term harm) · 3 Moderate (clinical decision delayed/altered, short-term harm) · 4 Major (serious harm, e.g. inappropriate surgery or cancellation with lasting effect) · 5 Catastrophic.
**Score = L × S.** Bands: 1–4 Low · 5–9 Medium · 10–14 High · 15–25 Very high.
**Residual** is shown as `L×S=score (band)` **after the planned controls work as designed**. It is unverified: no control has test evidence. Acceptability criteria: `<TBD by CSO>`.
Control tags: **SW** = software control; **PROC** = clinical/organisational procedure (see section 4). Status key: Open · Controls planned · Needs workshop · Needs NHS-side control (DCB0160).

Note on severity: this module's output feeds MDT discussion, not directly a treatment. Severity is mainly harm via an altered or delayed surgical/anaesthetic plan (3–4), and 5 only where a fall or cardiac event during testing is considered (PPA-H13).

## 5. UI safety prompts: what to include and what to avoid

### 5.1 Should contain (draft text for clinical review; not approved)

1. **Neutral results statement** on every results view and export: "This value was derived from times or answers entered or marked by a clinician. It is not an interpretation. The clinician is responsible for the test, its validity and any use of the result." **[External review]**
2. **Pre-test (clinician-asserted, not defaulted):** a checkbox "I have judged this patient suitable to attempt this test, and the local procedure is being followed", audited as an enumerated event. No eligibility logic.
3. **During test:** a persistent "Stop test" control; the reason picker offers enumerated codes; the 6MWT screen shows a clinician-approved stop-criteria reminder.
4. **Marking:** the exact frame time and index for each marker; the reported timing resolution; the live stopwatch time and the difference; a reminder of the start and end definitions for the selected protocol version.
5. **Confirmation:** a summary showing patient banner, marked frames, result with units, deviations, outcome, protocol/scoring version; a required confirmation action that is separate from marking.
6. **Deviation and validity:** an explicit "protocol followed / deviations (select)" choice and a "valid performance" assertion.
7. **Display:** units; date and age; amended/superseded badges; deviation badges; "Estimate" label on VO2peak; comparability explanation when a point is not plotted.
8. **Source availability:** if the video is gone, "Source video no longer available; result cannot be re-checked".
9. **Environment:** synthetic-data banner (core H20).

### 5.2 Should not contain

- Words or visuals that categorise or judge: normal/abnormal, good/poor, low/high risk, frail/pre-frail/robust, fit/unfit, "fit for surgery", pass/fail, red/amber/green, thresholds or reference lines, percentile/age-norm comparison, "improving/declining" trend labels, arrows implying direction of benefit.
- Any recommendation, alert, nudge, "consider…" prompts, auto-suggested tests, auto-suggested actions.
- Urgency or pressure language ("hurry", "keep going", countdown that implies a pass mark), or sounds/animations that encourage continuing a test.
- Defaulted answers for suitability, valid performance, deviations, consent or identity.
- More numeric precision than the timing method supports.
- Free text containing patient names or identifiers in notes that flow to audit/logs (notes live in the domain table only, never in audit metadata; core rule 5).
- Claims such as "validated", "accurate", "clinically proven", "compliant", "secure", "certified".

### 5.3 Standing statement (to be shown in-product and in user information)

> **Safety prompts and on-screen guidance in PreVid do not replace clinical judgement, the clinician's own training, or local policy and procedure. They are aids to recording, not instructions to test, to continue a test, or to act on a result. The responsible clinician decides whether and how a test is performed, whether a result is valid, and how it is used.**
